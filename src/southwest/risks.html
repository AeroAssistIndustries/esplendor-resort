Risks

What could change the outcome

A closed resort is a renovation project, not a running business. These are the main risks a buyer or partner should underwrite, and what is already in place for each.

RiskWhat could happenHow it is addressed

No operating history

Closed since 2015. Stabilizing after reopening may take longer than modeled, or may not happen.

Operated as a resort for 45 years; full plan set and line-item budget in hand; the returns model includes a conservative case.

Construction cost and timing

The $11–13M estimate is the owner’s; the 2023 appraisal carried $14.8M. The 18-month schedule may slip.

Plans are fire-department approved and ready to bid; Path B’s $15M includes $2–4M of reserves.

Room count

Sources differ: 179 (2023 appraisal), about 195 (CAD plans), 204 (owner presentation).

The owner is certifying the unit mix against the plan set before diligence.

Dated appraisal

Values are as of May 2023, prepared for the owner, and may not be relied on by others.

Buyers should commission their own; Hermosa’s final approval and the occupancy growth came after it.

Financing

No bank financing is committed; the 2024 USDA pre-approval was to the current owner and does not transfer.

Seller financing is available to qualified buyers under Path A.

Market concentration

Demand leans on border trade and the Hermosa mine, whose timeline is the operator’s and may slip.

Border trade and federal demand don’t depend on the mine, and county occupancy is already rising.

Thin comparables

Most Nogales hotel sales never publish a price; exit cap rates are uncertain.

Tucson trades and replacement cost give outside reference points (see hotel sales).

Add-on approvals

Glamping and solar canopies need county rulings; for-sale housing would need rezoning.

The hotel, extended stay, casitas and events are permitted by right under B-2 (see land potential).

Physical condition

Parts of the property remain in shell condition.

Phase I ESA available, roofs complete; buyers inspect independently.

Illiquidity and loss

Real estate, and any Path B interest, is illiquid. Investors could lose everything they put in.

Read the full offering documents and get independent advice before committing.
