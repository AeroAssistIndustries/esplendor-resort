What could go there

Seven add-on ideas

Programs are illustrative and not engineered. Each zoning note quotes the Santa Cruz County code. Items marked “ask the county” need a ruling from Community Development.

Area 1 · North rim · ≈1.3 ac

Extended-stay suites

A two-story building of kitchen suites for multi-week stays: mine contractors, border agencies, produce buyers and traveling nurses. It would run off the main resort's front desk and laundry.

Illustrative: 24–36 suites

Allowed by right as a hotel use · ask the county to confirm extended stay

  • Santa Cruz County hotels ran 58.0% occupancy at a $97.83 average rate in Q1 2026, up 8.8% year on year, with no new rooms added.[3]
  • The nearest branded extended-stay, in Tucson, starts at $113 a night.[9]

Area 2 · Southeast knoll · ≈2.4 ac

View casitas

Freestanding one- and two-bedroom casitas on the knoll facing the valley, sold as the resort's premium keys. They could also be built to sell as fractional or branded residences, which would need a zoning change.

Illustrative: 10–14 casitas

Allowed by right as hotel rooms · residences for sale would need a rezoning or PAD

  • Patagonia Lake State Park's basic cabins rent for $119 a night.[11]
  • Pima County hotels averaged $179.83 a night in Q1 2026.[3]

Area 3 · South slope · ≈1.9 ac

Event lawn and wedding pavilion

A terraced ceremony lawn and an open-sided pavilion below the ballroom, so one wedding can hold its ceremony outside and its reception in the 650-seat ballroom.

Illustrative: lawn for 250 guests and a covered pavilion

Venue-type uses (auditorium, club) allowed by right · a bar or cocktail lounge needs a use permit

  • Tubac Golf Resort wedding packages run $4,200–$20,000 plus tax, with meals from $96 a guest.[13]
  • Tucson venues typically cost $9,000–$11,100.[14]

Area 4 · Resort core

Wine tasting room

A tasting room in the main building or by the event lawn, pouring Sonoita and Willcox wines. It gives wine-country visitors a reason to stay on the I-19 side of the valley.

Illustrative: tasting bar inside an existing building

Allowed by right: B-2 carries the B-1 “farm winery … with or without a tasting room” use

  • Sonoita, Arizona's first wine region, now has 24 tasting rooms.[15]
  • Arizona wine tourists spent $241M in 2023, and about 46% came from out of state.[16]

Area 5 · West overlook · ≈0.8 ac

Glamping tents

A handful of canvas safari tents on platforms along the west edge, with a short overlook trail. They are cheap to build and easy to take down if the land is needed later.

Illustrative: 8–12 tents

Ask the county: glamping is not defined in the code

  • Glamping near Tubac averages about $90 a night on Hipcamp.[12]
  • RV sites at De Anza RV Resort in Amado rent for $75–$85 a night.[10]
  • An RV park would need a use permit, and a 2021 county case cites a 2-acre minimum.[2]

Area 6 · Northeast service pad · ≈0.4 ac

Staff housing and solar

A few staff apartments next to the maintenance building, which makes hiring easier in a tight rental market. Solar canopies over the east parking lot could cut the resort's power bill.

Illustrative: 4–8 staff units and parking-lot solar

Staff and caretaker housing allowed by right · ask the county about solar canopies

  • Local reporting in 2022 described year-long apartment waitlists and almost no rentals in Sonoita.[7]

Area 7 · Street lots · APN 114-50-013B (0.59 ac) and 114-50-176 (0.66 ac)

Commercial pads, or sell them

The two small parcels sit apart from the resort. A buyer could build a café or convenience store for the neighborhood, or sell the lots to bring down the net purchase price.

Illustrative: one small retail or café pad per lot

Restaurants, cafés and retail allowed by right

  • Small commercial lots near I-19 in Río Rico ask about $296,000–$389,000 an acre.[17]
  • Commercial land listed across Santa Cruz County averages about $113,000 an acre.[18]

Before you plan

What a buyer should verify

  1. Topographic survey. Most of the open land is slope. A survey sets the buildable share of each area.
  2. Zoning letter. Ask the county to confirm extended stay as a hotel use and rule on glamping and solar canopies. Also check whether Article 20, the Río Rico Community District, applies.
  3. Utilities. Confirm water, sewer and power capacity for added rooms with Liberty Utilities and the county.
  4. Access and fire. Hillside sites need fire-truck access and turnarounds that meet the fire code.
  5. Street-lot title. Confirm that APN 114-50-013B and 114-50-176 can be sold or built on separately.
